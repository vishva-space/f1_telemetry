================================================================================
                    HANDOVER SPECIFICATION: CdA & VEHICLE PARAMETERS
================================================================================

OBJECTIVE
---------
Calculate the car's Drag Area (CdA) and other key drivetrain parameters at its 
maximum speed, using a power balance, weather‑corrected air density, and a 
properly normalised wind component derived from the car's heading.

CORE ASSUMPTIONS (Fixed Values)
-------------------------------
- Engine Power (P_engine)      = 750,000 W   (Assumed peak ICE power)
- Drivetrain Efficiency (η)    = 0.95        (95% efficiency from engine to wheels)
- Wheel Radius (r_wheel)       = 0.355 m     (Typical F1 tire radius)
- Specific Gas Constant (R)    = 287.05
- Coordinate System            : X+ = North, Y+ = East (from location.csv)

CORE EQUATIONS
--------------

1. AIR DENSITY (ρ)
   ρ = (P_hPa × 100) / (287.05 × (T_C + 273.15))
   Where:
     - P_hPa = Air pressure (hPa) from weather data
     - T_C   = Air temperature (°C) from weather data

2. CAR HEADING (θ_car)
   The car's heading is derived from two consecutive position points 
   (location.csv) that bracket the timestamp of maximum speed.

   Δx = x_{i+1} - x_i
   Δy = y_{i+1} - y_i
   θ_car = atan2(Δy, Δx)   [radians, measured clockwise from North (X+ axis)]

   Note: The location data must be filtered to ensure the car is on a straight 
   section (small change in heading) to avoid curvature errors.

3. HEADWIND COMPONENT (v_wind_head)
   The meteorological wind direction (wind_direction) is defined as the 
   direction FROM which the wind blows (e.g., 180° = South wind, blows northward).
   The wind vector (direction it blows TOWARDS) is:
     θ_wind = wind_direction + 180°  (mod 360°)

   The angle between the car's heading and the wind vector:
     Δθ = θ_wind - θ_car   [radians]

   The effective headwind component (positive = headwind, negative = tailwind):
     v_wind_head = wind_speed × cos(Δθ)

   Therefore:
     v_rel = v_car + v_wind_head

   where v_car = speed at max point (m/s) = speed_kph / 3.6

4. CdA (Drag Area)
   CdA = (2 × P_wheels) / (ρ × v_rel³)
   Where P_wheels = P_engine × η = 750,000 × 0.95 = 712,500 W

5. ENGINE ANGULAR VELOCITY (ω_engine)
   ω_engine = RPM × (2π / 60)    [rad/s]

6. ENGINE TORQUE (τ_engine)
   τ_engine = P_engine / ω_engine    [Nm]

7. OVERALL GEAR RATIO at Max Speed (i_overall)
   i_overall = (RPM × 2π × r_wheel) / (v_mps × 60)

8. WHEEL TORQUE (τ_wheel)
   τ_wheel = τ_engine × i_overall × η    [Nm]

9. AVERAGE GEAR RATIOS (for each gear used)
   For each gear number (n_gear), calculate the average Overall Ratio
   across all valid telemetry rows where that gear is engaged.
   Formula: Avg_i_gear = Average( RPM × 2π × r_wheel / (speed_mps × 60) )
   (Rows with missing RPM, speed=0, or gear=0/null are excluded)

INPUTS REQUIRED
--------------------------------
| Source         | Columns Used                                     |
|----------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| car_data       | speed, rpm, n_gear, date                         |
| weather        | air_temperature, pressure, wind_speed,           |
|                | wind_direction, meeting_key, session_key, date   |
| location       | x, y, date (to compute heading)                  |

PROCESS OVERVIEW (Step by Step)
-------------------------------
1. Convert speed to m/s.
2. Compute Overall Ratio for every row: (RPM × 2π × 0.355) / (speed_mps × 60)
3. Filter out invalid rows (nulls, zeros).
4. Group by gear and calculate the average Overall Ratio per gear.
5. Find the row with the maximum speed value in car_data.
6. Extract RPM, speed, gear, meeting_key, session_key, and timestamp from that row.
7. Match to the closest weather record (by timestamp) for the same meeting/session.
8. Match to the closest location records before and after the max speed timestamp.
9. Compute car heading from the location points (see Equation 2).
10. Compute the headwind component using wind_speed and wind_direction (see Equation 3).
11. Calculate relative speed: v_rel = max_speed_mps + v_wind_head.
12. Calculate air density (ρ) from pressure and temperature.
13. Calculate CdA using the power balance equation (see Equation 4).
14. Calculate engine torque, wheel torque, and overall ratio at max speed.
15. Compile all outputs into a single table.

OUTPUTS GENERATED (Copy of your sheet list)
-------------------------------------------
1.  CdA (m²)
2.  Max Speed (kph)
3.  Max Speed (m/s)
4.  Max RPM
5.  Max Gear
6.  Engine Torque at Max Speed (Nm)
7.  Wheel Torque at Max Speed (Nm)
8.  Overall Ratio at Max Speed
9.  Air Density (kg/m³)
10. Gear 2 Overall Ratio        (average ratio for 2nd gear)
11. Gear 3 Overall Ratio        (average ratio for 3rd gear)
12. Gear 4 Overall Ratio        (average ratio for 4th gear)
13. Gear 5 Overall Ratio        (average ratio for 5th gear)
14. Gear 6 Overall Ratio        (average ratio for 6th gear)
15. Gear 7 Overall Ratio        (average ratio for 7th gear)
16. Gear 8 Overall Ratio        (average ratio for 8th gear)

Note: Gear 1 is omitted as it is typically not used at maximum speed and may
      have insufficient data for a reliable average.

KEY ASSUMPTIONS & LIMITATIONS (Important to document)
-----------------------------------------------------
- P_engine is fixed at 750kW. Real ICE power varies with RPM, fuel flow, and mode.
- Rolling resistance is neglected. At high speed, drag dominates, but this
  introduces a small overestimation of CdA.
- The wind is normalised using the car's heading derived from GPS location data.
  This removes the need to assume a direct headwind; the component along the
  car's direction is used correctly.
- Tire radius is fixed at 0.355m. Real tires grow with speed and deflect under load.
- Steady-state is assumed at max speed (no acceleration, flat track).
- Air density uses standard dry-air gas constant; humidity is not accounted for.
- The coordinate system for location data must be X+ = North, Y+ = East.
- The wind direction is assumed to be meteorological (direction from which the wind originates).

DATA QUALITY NOTES
------------------
- The DRS column is present in car_data but currently empty; it is ignored.
- The location (x, y, z) data is used only for heading calculation; z is not used.
- Timestamps are used to match weather data to the exact moment of max speed.
- For heading computation, only location points that are on a straight section
  (i.e., minimal change in heading over the interval) should be used to avoid
  curvature errors.

MASS VALIDATION CHECK
================================================================================

At maximum speed, acceleration is zero. The force balance is:

    F_trac = F_drag + F_rr

Where:
    F_trac = P_wheels / v_max
    F_drag = 0.5 × ρ × CdA × v_max²
    F_rr   = C_rr × m × g

Since the car's mass is constrained by regulations (minimum 768 kg + driver + fuel),
we can use this equation to validate the calculated CdA.

If we assume a realistic mass for qualifying (~860 kg) and a realistic rolling
resistance coefficient for F1 tires (C_rr ≈ 0.018–0.025), the residual force
(F_trac - F_drag) must fall within this range.

Validation Rules:
    1. If F_trac - F_drag < 0          → CdA is TOO HIGH (impossible top speed)
    2. If F_trac - F_drag > 0.025 × m × g → CdA is TOO LOW (unrealistic rolling resistance)
    3. If F_trac - F_drag is negative or requires C_rr > 0.035 → CdA must be adjusted.

This check requires no additional input data. It uses only the outputs already
calculated (CdA, F_trac, air density) and the known regulatory minimum mass.

Equation for the sanity check:

    C_rr_calculated = (F_trac - F_drag) / (m_assumed × g)

Where m_assumed should be a realistic value for the session being analyzed
(~860 kg for qualifying, ~900+ kg for race laps).
