================================================================================
                    HANDOVER SPECIFICATION: ENGINE POWER LOGIC
================================================================================

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.  FIXED VEHICLE CONSTANTS (to be defined per session)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Parameter                Symbol       Value / Source
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Air Density              rho          Calculated from weather data (pressure & temp)
Drag Area                CdA          From the CdA handover (derived per session)
Car Mass                 m            Session‑dependent: qualifying (~830 kg) or race
Rolling Resistance Coeff Cr           Assumption (tunable, typically 0.012–0.020)
Tyre Rolling Radius      r            Derived from max speed point
Drivetrain Efficiency    eta          0.95 (fixed assumption)
Thermal Efficiency       eta_thermal  0.46 (fixed assumption)
Gravity                  g            9.81 m/s² (constant)

Gear Ratios (Overall) – derived from car data (see CdA handover):
  Gear 2   R2 = <calculated>
  Gear 3   R3 = <calculated>
  Gear 4   R4 = <calculated>
  Gear 5   R5 = <calculated>
  Gear 6   R6 = <calculated>
  Gear 7   R7 = <calculated>
  Gear 8   R8 = <calculated>

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.  DATA ACQUISITION & ALIGNMENT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Retrieve from Fast F1 API:
  a) Car Telemetry:    date, speed (kph), rpm, n_gear, throttle (%), brake (0/100)
  b) Weather:          date, air_temperature, pressure, wind_speed, wind_direction
  c) Location:         date, x, y

Data Alignment:
  - For each car telemetry row, match the nearest weather and location records by
    timestamp (nearest‑time merge).
  - Convert speed: v (m/s) = speed (kph) / 3.6

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.  DATA FILTERING RULES (Pre‑processing)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
a) Accept only rows where: Throttle == 100  AND  Brake == 0   (full power)
b) Reject rows where:      n_gear == 0
c) Reject transients:      discard points where RPM drops > 500 within 0.2 s
                           (post-upshift)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.  STEP‑BY‑STEP CALCULATION LOGIC (for each valid datapoint)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Step 1 – Kinematics:
  omega_w = v / r                               (wheel angular speed, rad/s)
  omega_e = (RPM * 2 * pi) / 60                  (engine angular speed, rad/s)

Step 2 – Acceleration (central difference):
  a = (v_{i+1} - v_{i-1}) / (t_{i+1} - t_{i-1}) (m/s²)
  (For first/last rows, use forward/backward difference)

Step 3 – Tractive Power at Wheels:
  F_drag   = 0.5 * rho * CdA * v²
  F_rr     = Cr * m * g
  F_inert  = m * a
  P_w      = (F_drag + F_rr + F_inert) * v      (Watts)

Step 4 – Total Crankshaft Torque (ICE + MGU‑K):
  T_total = P_w / (omega_e * eta)               (Nm)

Step 5 – MGU‑K Maximum Power (Art. C5.2.8):
  This depends on the session type (Overtake Mode active in qualifying).
  For qualifying, assume Overtake active:
      P_MGU_max = 7100 - 20 * v                 (v in kph)
      P_MGU_max = min(P_MGU_max, 350)           (cap at 350 kW)
      If v > 355 kph: P_MGU_max = 0

  For race/sprint, use the appropriate non‑Overtake curve.

Step 6 – MGU‑K Torque:
  T_MGU = (P_MGU_max * 1000) / omega_e          (Nm)
  Clamp per Art. C5.2.11:
      T_MGU = max( T_MGU, -500 )
      T_MGU = min( T_MGU,  500 )                (must be between -500 and +500)

Step 7 – Isolate ICE Torque:
  T_ICE = T_total - T_MGU                       (Nm)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.  FUEL FLOW CAPPING (Validation / Clipping)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 8 – Maximum Fuel Energy Flow (Art. C5.2.3 & C5.2.4):
  EF_max = min( 3000,  0.27 * RPM + 165 )       (MJ/h)
  (Linear term applies below 10,500 rpm; above, the 3000 cap dominates)

Step 9 – Maximum ICE Power from Fuel:
  P_ICE_max = (EF_max / 3.6) * eta_thermal      (kW)

Step 10 – Maximum ICE Torque from Fuel:
  T_ICE_max = (P_ICE_max * 1000) / omega_e      (Nm)

Step 11 – Apply Cap (if required):
  If T_ICE > T_ICE_max  then  T_ICE = T_ICE_max

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.  ENGINE BRAKING / NEGATIVE TORQUE FLOOR (Art. C5.12.3)
    (Applied only when Throttle == 0  AND  Brake == 100)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 12 – Regulatory Minimum Torque:
  T_min = -0.0027 * RPM - 30                    (Nm)
  (This is the most negative torque permitted; the actual demand must be
   greater – i.e., less negative – than this value)

Step 13 – Harvesting Availability Check (Art. C5.2.10):
  The MGU‑K may harvest up to 8.5 MJ per lap (or 9.0 MJ with Overtake).
  If the harvested energy in the current lap has already reached this limit:
      T_min = 0                                  (no regen / no engine braking)

Step 14 – Applied Negative Torque:
  T_demand_braking = T_min + 0.5                (Nm)
  (The +0.5 Nm ensures compliance by staying safely above the floor)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.  DRIVER DEMAND MAPPING (for any Throttle position, 0% – 100%)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Step 15 – Base ICE Torque from Full‑Throttle Map:
  Fit a 4th‑order polynomial to the (RPM, T_ICE) data points from Step 7:
      T_ICE_base = c0 + c1*RPM + c2*RPM² + c3*RPM³ + c4*RPM⁴

Step 16 – MGU‑K Torque at current speed:
  Use Step 5 and Step 6 to compute T_MGU based on current v and mode.

Step 17 – Total Available Torque at 100% Throttle:
  T_available = T_ICE_base + T_MGU              (Nm)

Step 18 – Scaled by Driver Demand:
  T_demand = (Throttle / 100) * T_available     (Nm)

Step 19 – Apply Engine Braking Floor (if off‑throttle):
  If Throttle == 0 and Brake == 100:
      T_demand = min( T_demand,  T_min + 0.5 )  (cannot be more negative)

Step 20 – Final Output:
  T_output = T_demand                           (Nm)
  This is the net torque at the engine crankshaft that the powertrain
  controller will target.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8.  VALIDATION CHECK (at the maximum speed point of the session)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Run this sanity check on the row where speed is highest. Use the actual values
from that row (v_max, RPM, gear) and the constants defined above.

Calculate:
  Drag force     = 0.5 * rho * CdA * v_max²
  Rolling resist. = Cr * m * g
  Tractive force = Drag + Rolling resist. (assuming a ≈ 0 at top speed)
  Wheel power    = Tractive force * v_max
  Total engine torque = (Wheel power * 1000) / (omega_e * eta)
  MGU‑K torque   = (P_MGU_max * 1000) / omega_e   (using the power cap for that speed)
  Derived ICE torque = Total engine torque – MGU‑K torque

Then check against fuel cap:
  At the given RPM, compute EF_max and T_ICE_max.
  Ensure Derived ICE torque < T_ICE_max (if not, the constants or data are inconsistent).

Mass Sensitivity:
  Re‑run the validation with mass ± 5% to confirm stability of the derived ICE torque.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9.  ENERGY MANAGEMENT & BATTERY CAPACITY (Art. C5.2.9 & C5.2.10)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The regulations impose two key limits on electrical energy:

  a) Per‑Lap Recharge Limit (C5.2.10): The MGU‑K may harvest a maximum of 8.5 MJ
     per lap (or 9.0 MJ with Overtake active). This caps the total electrical
     energy that can be deployed in a lap.

  b) State of Charge Swing (C5.2.9): The difference between the maximum and
     minimum state of charge of the Energy Store must not exceed 4 MJ at any
     time the car is on track.

Critical Implication for the Model:
  The total physical capacity of the battery is irrelevant for torque
  calculations. The 4 MJ limit is a "sliding window" on the battery's state of
  charge. It forces the car to constantly oscillate between deploying and
  harvesting energy within each lap, rather than storing large amounts for later.

Energy Tracking Logic (to be implemented in the script):
  The code does NOT need a "battery percentage" or "total capacity" variable.
  Instead, it should track the net cumulative energy over the current lap:

    net_energy = energy_deployed - energy_harvested

  The MGU‑K deployment logic must ensure that net_energy never exceeds 4 MJ
  (i.e., the battery state of charge never drops more than 4 MJ below its peak).
  When this limit is reached, deployment is paused until harvesting occurs.

  In practice, the MGU‑K power curve (Step 5) already limits instantaneous
  power to 350 kW. The 4 MJ window simply limits how long that power can be
  sustained before recharging is required.

What This Means for the Torque Calculation:
  The instantaneous torque at any given moment depends ONLY on the speed-
  dependent MGU‑K power limit (Step 5). The battery capacity does not appear
  anywhere in the torque equations. The 4 MJ window acts as a logical
  constraint on deployment duration, not on instantaneous force output.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. OUTPUT DATA STRUCTURE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The script shall append the following calculated columns to the filtered dataset:

  omega_e        Engine angular speed (rad/s)
  accel          Longitudinal acceleration (m/s²)
  P_w            Wheel tractive power (W)
  T_total        Total crankshaft torque (ICE + MGU‑K) (Nm)
  P_MGU          MGU‑K electrical power (W)
  T_MGU          MGU‑K mechanical torque (clamped) (Nm)
  T_ICE_capped   Final ICE torque (after fuel capping) (Nm)
  EF_max         Maximum fuel energy flow (MJ/h)
  net_energy     Cumulative net energy (deployed - harvested) over current lap (MJ)

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. IMPLEMENTATION NOTES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- The Fast F1 API returns asynchronous data streams. Align weather and location
  to car telemetry using nearest‑time matching.
- Session type (qualifying/race) determines which MGU‑K power curve to use.
- Rolling resistance coefficient (Cr) is tunable; start with 0.012 and adjust if
  validation shows excessive residual force.
- Gear 1 is excluded from all calculations.
- Use vectorised operations (Pandas/Numpy) rather than explicit loops for
  efficient processing of high‑frequency data.
- The battery capacity is NOT a required input. The 4 MJ state‑of‑charge swing
  (C5.2.9) and 8.5 MJ per‑lap recharge limit (C5.2.10) are the only energy
  constraints that affect the model.
